Most people hear "impermanence" and think of death, breakups, loss. Everything passes. Sounds sad.

But that's not what impermanence actually means.

> Impermanence doesn't mean things *will* change. It means things *are* changing — right now, in every moment.

The hand holding this article isn't the same hand from two seconds ago. Same shape, but millions of cells have shifted. The thought that just passed through your mind is already gone.

Not "everything will eventually pass" — but "everything is in flow right now."

## Why this matters

Because most of our suffering comes from trying to hold onto what's already moving. Wanting good feelings to last. Wanting to avoid difficult ones. Wanting people to stay as they are. Wanting ourselves not to change.

But if everything is moving according to its own nature — then suffering is swimming against the current.

> Impermanence isn't something that happens *to* you. It's the nature of reality — including "you."

<div class="practice">
<div class="practice-label">Today's observation</div>

Look at it and ask: "How is this changing right now — even slightly?"

No need to see anything dramatic. Just notice.
