Honest question 2 min read

When you say "I'm angry" — *who's angry?*

And who is aware of the anger?

Yesterday I got annoyed at a colleague for sending a late email. Not the first time. I felt irritated — and right then, a question appeared: Who is angry?

The question sounds silly. Obviously I’m the one who’s angry. But try stopping and actually asking it.

When you say “I’m angry” — there are two things present: the anger itself, and the awareness of the anger.

The anger is loud, hot, wants to do something. But the part that knows about the anger — the part observing “oh, I’m angry” — that part is much quieter. And it isn’t angry.

Two things at once

Try noticing next time you have a strong emotion. Anger, anxiety, sadness. In the middle of that emotion, there’s a part of you observing it. That part isn’t swept away. That part is still here.

This isn’t theory. This is something you can check right now.

The anger is a cloud. Awareness is the sky. However thick the clouds get, the sky is still there.

This doesn’t mean “don’t be angry.” Anger is natural. Emotions don’t need to be eliminated.

But when you recognize that you aren’t the anger — but rather the awareness of the anger — a small gap appears. And in that gap, there’s choice.

Practice — 30 seconds

Right now, notice what you’re feeling. Even if it’s neutral, slightly anxious, or tired.

Then ask: “Who is aware of this?”

No need to answer. Just ask and notice what happens.

You are not the wave.
You are the ocean watching the wave.
