5-min practice 2 min read

Try *not preparing* your reply in one meeting.

Just listen. See what happens.

In yesterday’s meeting, I tried a small experiment. Instead of listening to my colleague while simultaneously preparing a reply — I just listened. No preparation at all.

The feeling was strange enough to be funny. For a second I panicked: What if it’s my turn and I have nothing to say?

But when my turn came, the response appeared. And it was better than usual — because it actually responded to what had just been said, rather than what I’d pre-prepared.

Most of us don’t listen — we’re waiting for our turn to speak.

This isn’t our fault. That’s how the mind works when anxious. It wants to control, prepare, avoid being caught off guard.

What does real listening look like?

Real listening means letting information in before a reaction goes out. Listening means being present with the person speaking — not with the voice in your own head.

When you truly listen, the other person feels it. No need to say anything special — just being genuinely present is already a rare gift.

Presence requires no skill. It only requires letting go — letting go of what’s being prepared in your head.

Practice today

In your next meeting or conversation — choose a 5-minute stretch to just listen.

When you notice yourself preparing a reply, gently return to listening. No self-judgment. Just return.

Afterward, notice: did the conversation feel different?

The best listener in the room
usually isn't the one who talks the most.
